## I build at the intersection of AI, product and infrastructure

I am a Nigerian-born, London-based founder, product engineer and **Google AI Fellow 2026**. I founded [Amakora](https://amakoragroup.com), an applied AI company building products that make powerful AI systems more useful, governable and accessible.

My work sits between customer need and deep technical execution: translating real organisational problems into product strategy, platform architecture and working software. Before Amakora, I led platform and developer ecosystem work across **Interswitch** and **RedCloud**. My wider perspective has also been shaped by study at **Stanford Graduate School of Business**.

I am especially interested in the layer between foundation models and enterprise outcomes: the **AI harness** that connects models to context, tools, memory, policy, evaluation, observability and human judgement.

> Better models expand what is possible. Better systems determine what becomes dependable.

## What I am studying

My current work explores five connected questions:

- **AI harnesses and control planes:** How should organisations orchestrate models, tools, context and policy without becoming locked into one provider?
- **Evaluation and reliability:** How do we measure quality, safety, cost and outcome drift continuously—not only before launch?
- **Agentic systems:** When should software retrieve, reason, act, ask for approval or hand control back to a person?
- **Developer experience:** How can APIs, SDKs, CLIs, MCP and documentation make sophisticated systems easier to adopt?
- **AI adoption:** What operating model helps enterprises move from scattered pilots to governed, valuable capability?

I turn this study into products, architecture, essays, practical guides and teaching.

## Principles behind my work

1. **Start with the outcome.** Technology matters when it changes what a person or organisation can accomplish.
2. **Make trust architectural.** Evaluation, policy, observability and human oversight belong in the system—not in a later compliance layer.
3. **Design for portability.** Models will change; durable products preserve choice at the infrastructure layer.
4. **Teach what you learn.** Clear explanation is part of building inclusive, high-adoption technology.
5. **Be precise about evidence.** A prototype, a deployed candidate and a production system are not the same thing.
